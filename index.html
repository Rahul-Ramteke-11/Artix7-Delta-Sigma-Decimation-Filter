From Equation to Gate-Level Silicon

Oversampling & Pulse Density Modulation

The delta-sigma (ΔΣ) modulator front-end converts continuous analog voltages into a 1-bit pulse density modulated (PDM) bitstream at an extreme oversampling rate of 512 kHz.

Oversampling Ratio OSR = f_s / (2 · f_Nyquist) = 512,000 / (2 × 2,000) = 128

At OSR = 128, the quantization noise is pushed far above the signal band. The density of logic-high pulses in the bitstream is directly proportional to the instantaneous analog input voltage — a voltage of 0 V produces a 50% pulse density, while full-scale inputs approach 0% or 100%.

This single-bit approach eliminates the need for precision DACs and drastically simplifies the analog front-end, trading analog complexity for digital oversampling headroom.

Cascaded Integrator-Comb (CIC) Decimation

The CIC filter is a multiplier-free decimation architecture that uses only additions, subtractions, and register delays. Its transfer function can be expressed as:

CIC Transfer Function H(z) = [(1 - z^(-RM)) / (1 - z^(-1))]^N

where R = decimation ratio (128), M = differential delay (1), and N = filter order (number of cascaded stages). Each stage consists of an integrator section (running at the high sample rate) and a comb section (running at the decimated output rate).

Because the CIC architecture replaces all multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations (A × B) with simple accumulators and differentiators, it consumes 0 out of 90 DSP48E1 slices on the Artix-7 — saving 100% of the FPGA's dedicated multiplier resources for other IP cores.
